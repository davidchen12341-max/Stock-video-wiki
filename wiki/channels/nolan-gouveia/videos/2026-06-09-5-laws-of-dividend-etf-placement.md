**Thesis:** Account placement — which dividend ETF sits in taxable vs. traditional IRA vs. Roth IRA — matters as much as fund selection and costs retirees five to six figures over 20 years.

**Key takeaways:**
- **Law 1 — SCHD goes anywhere:** Qualified dividend treatment means favorable tax rates in any account; most flexible
- **Law 2 — JEPI and JEPQ belong in tax-advantaged accounts:** Covered-call distributions are ordinary income — extremely inefficient in a taxable brokerage; the "$7,000 JEPI Tax Trap" framing
- **Law 3 — SPYI and QQQI belong in taxable brokerage:** Return-of-capital structure defers taxes, making them ideal outside a tax-advantaged wrapper
- **Law 4 — VYMI and the foreign tax credit:** International dividend ETF creates a foreign tax credit only claimable in a taxable account — putting it in an IRA wastes the credit
- **Law 5 — Bonds belong in tax-advantaged accounts:** Ordinary income from bond interest gets sheltered inside IRA/Roth
- Dollar math segment (10:46) shows the compounding difference this placement strategy creates

**Tickers mentioned:** SCHD, JEPI, JEPQ, SPYI, QQQI, VYMI

**Notable calls:** JEPI/JEPQ should "almost never" sit in a taxable brokerage — strong, actionable rule. SPYI/QQQI's return-of-capital structure is a placement advantage most creators skip.
