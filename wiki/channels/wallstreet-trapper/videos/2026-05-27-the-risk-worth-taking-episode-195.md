# The Risk Worth Taking | Episode 195

**Thesis:** Trapper's memory-sector and space basket thesis is playing out live — SNDK, STX, and MU are all printing while he waits for ASTS and RKLB to confirm entries; he's explicitly bearish on buying SpaceX at IPO, calling the $1.75T valuation on $18B revenue "way overvalued."

## Key Takeaways

- **Memory sector is the core thesis:** SNDK, STX, MU, WDC, and TSM make up the backbone of both the options portfolio and the buy-and-hold Freedom Index. MU alone produced $47K on a single options play (30 contracts entered, trimmed to ~5). Community was panicking on STX when it dipped — Trapper held at technical support (pin bar on key level), it bounced.
- **Space basket incoming — but not yet:** Planet Labs (PL) is the current space position ("my introduction to space"). ASTS and Rocket Labs (RKLB) are flagged explicitly as "coming soon" — not yet entered but on the immediate watchlist.
- **SpaceX IPO: Bearish at open.** IPO date June 12. Trapper won't chase: "It's going to run up and then once them people can get liquid, they going to get liquid." Core math: $1.75T valuation on $18B revenue = ~1000x multiple vs. Amazon's 10x. Plans to wait for the post-lock-up selloff before considering entry.
- **SpaceX/Tesla merger speculation:** CNBC clip discussed — legal experts say no antitrust issue but valuation questions remain. Musk needs SpaceX at $7.5T for his performance bonus; a merger gets to ~$3.5T. Trapper frames it as interesting but no actionable trade right now.
- **Freedom Index up 32% YTD** (vs. ~10% market): Holdings are Costco, EME, FIX, ITA, MU, Nvidia, PWR, STX, TPL. Claims 4 consecutive years of double-digit outperformance. Options profits are taken and recycled into this buy-and-hold index.
- **Market macro view:** $6 trillion sitting on sidelines — institutions buy every dip at key levels. AI now drives ~90% of US GDP growth via hyperscaler infrastructure build-out. Current playbook: wait for pullbacks to dynamic support levels, enter there, don't fight the trend.
